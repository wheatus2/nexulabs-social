## 1. NHS England's guidance on missed hospital appointments says to send reminders and make it easy to cancel
- Used on: caption only ("NHS England's guidance on missed hospital appointments says the same: send reminders, and make it easy to cancel.").
- Source: NHS England, "Reducing did not attends (DNAs) in outpatient services". https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/reducing-did-not-attends-dnas-in-outpatient-services/
- As the source states it (Introduction): "As a minimum, all providers should use appointment reminders (e.g. via SMS) and offer patients an easy option to cancel..." Under "Improving contact access and cancellation/rebooking processes": "It is important to allow patients to cancel or rearrange their appointments easily."
- Date: the page itself shows no publication date; it ends "Publication reference: PR2012". Two other pages date it to February 2023 (HTN, 16 Feb 2023; Royal College of Ophthalmologists, 22 Feb 2023). Checked 13 Nov 2026. No number from this page is used, so the 24-month rule for figures does not apply; it is cited only as guidance that is still published.
- Scope: England, hospital outpatient services ("providers"). It is guidance to the NHS, not to businesses; the caption says whose guidance it is and what it is about.

## 2. People forget appointments
- Used on: caption ("on the day it has gone clean out of their head") and the idea of the Reel. This is a scenario, not a statistic.
- Support: the same NHS England page, under "Causes of DNAs", lists among "Reasons for DNAs within patients' control": "Patient has forgotten about the appointment."

## 3. A booking can send its own reminder the day before
- Used on: scenes 2 and 3 and the caption ("Or the booking can do it: a text or an email goes out on its own, with a way to cancel or move the slot.").
- This is advice about how to set up a booking system or CRM, which is the kind of system Nexulabs builds. No product is named and no feature of any named product is claimed. "The day before" and "09:00" are our example of a setting, not a rule from any source.

## 4. The calendar and the dates
- The tear-off calendar is a drawing made for this post. It is not a real product.
- The dates are real 2026 dates: 13 November 2026 is a Friday, 3 December 2026 is a Thursday, 4 December 2026 is a Friday. 4 December is three weeks after 13 November, which is what the caption's "three weeks' time" refers to.

## Not claimed
