## 1. Use one or two fonts, two at most
- Used on: scene 3 ("Pick one. Two at most."), caption ("pick one or two fonts that work together", "So pick one. Two at most.").
- Source: Adobe Express (UK), "Try out these typography examples for stylish and readable designs." Page title: "Get creative with these typography examples | Adobe Express". https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/discover/examples/typography
- As the source states it: "Try not to use too many different fonts in one design, as this can make things appear cluttered or unprofessional." "Instead, select one or two fonts that work well together and use them consistently." "To keep your design looking polished, limit yourself to two fonts per project."
- Date: no publish date is shown on the page. Footer "Copyright © 2026 Adobe". Live and checked 12 Nov 2026.
- Scope: general design advice from Adobe, on its UK site. Not a statistic.

## 2. Use the same fonts everywhere, because consistency is how a brand gets recognised
- Used on: scene 2 ("Website. Van. Invoice. Socials. Do they match?"), scene 4 ("Now they match.", "Use it everywhere."), caption ("use the same ones across everything you put out", "it's harder for people to recognise them as the same business", "Then use it everywhere.").

- As the source states it: "Remember, consistency is crucial, so use the same fonts across all your materials to reinforce your brand or message." "Every brand needs to have some sort of style consistency in order to build brand recognition and trust."

- Checked 12 Nov 2026.

## 3. Apple tells app designers to keep the number of typefaces down, because mixing too many can hinder readability
- Used on: caption only ("Apple gives app designers similar advice: keep the number of typefaces down, because mixing too many can make things harder to read.").
- Source: Apple, Human Interface Guidelines, "Typography", under "Conveying hierarchy". https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography.md
- As the source states it: "Minimize the number of typefaces you use, even in a highly customized interface. Mixing too many different typefaces can obscure your information hierarchy and hinder readability, in addition to making an interface feel internally inconsistent or poorly designed."
- Date: the page's latest change-log entry is December 16, 2025. Live and checked 12 Nov 2026.
- Scope: Apple's guidance is written for people designing apps and interfaces, not for brands in general. The caption says "app designers".
