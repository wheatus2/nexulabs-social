## 1. A business may ask customers for Google reviews, and Google suggests a link or QR code
- Used on: frames 1 to 3 (the idea of asking for a review after a job), caption ("Google's own tip is to give customers a link or QR code to leave one.").
- Source: Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to get more reviews". https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en
- As the source states it: "Remind customers to leave reviews: To leave reviews, you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code."
- Date: no date shown on the page. Checked 12 Nov 2026.

## 2. Google does not allow asking only happy customers for reviews
- Used on: frame 3 ("Ask every customer, every time. Not only the happy ones."), caption ("Don't ask only the happy customers.").
- Source: Google, Maps User Generated Content Policy Help, "Prohibited & restricted content" (Rating Manipulation section). https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en
- As the source states it: "We do not allow merchants to:" ... "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". And what is allowed: "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."
- Date: no date shown on the page. Checked 12 Nov 2026.
- Note: Google's rule is a ban on selective asking; it does not require a business to ask everyone. The caption gives the rule as "Don't ask only the happy customers." Frame 3's "Ask every customer, every time" is our advice on how to stay inside it, and is not attributed to Google.

## 3. Google prohibits offering a discount or a freebie in return for a review
- Used on: caption ("And don't offer a discount or a freebie in return.").
- Source A: the same Google policy page as claim 2. "Offer incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services - in exchange for posting any review or revision or removal of a negative review." (listed under "We do not allow merchants to:").
- Source B: Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to get more reviews" (as claim 1). "Offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews, change reviews, or remove negative reviews is considered fake & misleading content and is strictly prohibited."
- Checked 12 Nov 2026.

## 4. The CMA's guidance for UK businesses that publish reviews lists reviews that hide a reward as banned content
- Used on: caption only ("The CMA's guidance for UK businesses that publish reviews also lists reviews that hide a reward as banned content.").
- Source: Competition and Markets Authority, "Short guide for businesses: publishing consumer reviews and complying with consumer protection law" (GOV.UK, guidance). https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law
- As the source states it: "banned content (namely fake reviews, concealed incentivised reviews and false or misleading consumer review information)". "concealed incentivised reviews: these are reviews that conceal the fact that they have been incentivised", "for example, not making it clear that a review has been written in exchange for money or other benefits".
- Date: "Published 4 April 2025". Checked 12 Nov 2026.
- Not legal advice, and the caption does not say what the law requires beyond what the guide lists. The short guide does not name the Act or a start date, so neither is in the post.
