# Sources: 2026-11-14-0837-lights-out

Checked on 14 November 2026. Every page below was found by web search and opened during this run.

**No day counts are published.** The first draft said "day 30", "day 90" and "day 95", which are Nominet's rules today. The independent fact-check found that Nominet moves .uk to a new registry platform on 9 February 2027 and shortens the lifecycle then, so a name that expires from now on would not get those numbers. The frames and caption now give the order of the stages, which holds before and after the change (see the Lifecycle Policy under claim 8), and the date of the change. A second check then corrected "first the website stops": the first stage is a short period in which the name can still work.

## Claim 1 (frame 1, caption): a .uk domain (a .co.uk is one) that isn't renewed goes in stages, not all at once
- Nominet, Registrar Resources, "Domain expiry process and introduction of drop lists for .UK"
  https://registrars.nominet.uk/uk-namespace/new-domain-expiry-process-and-introduction-of-drop-lists-for-uk/
  Nominet's own page for registrars. Page last modified 11 August 2026. Table rows: Expiry Grace Period ("Fully operational. Available to renew."), then Redemption Grace Period ("Does not resolve."), then Pending Delete Grace Period ("Not available to renew.").
- Nominet's consumer site, The UK Domain, "What happens if I don't renew my domain name by the expiry date?"
  https://theukdomain.uk/help-centre/what-happens-if-i-dont-renew-my-domain-name-by-the-expiry-date/
  No date shown. Lists the same stages: expiry grace period, redemption grace period, pending delete period, domain deletion. "Although your domain name has expired, it still exists, and you are able to renew it with your registrar."

## Claim 1b (frame 1, caption): it can stay on for a short while after expiry
- Nominet, Registrar Resources (first URL above). Expiry Grace Period row, the first stage: "Fully operational. Available to renew."
- "Can", not "will": The UK Domain help page says a registrar's own terms "may result in your domain name and associated services being suspended before the times stated below".
- After 9 February 2027: Nominet's Lifecycle Policy (see claim 8), section 7.5: "Registrars are not required by registry policy to interrupt the DNS resolution path during the auto-renew grace period of an expired domain."

## Claim 2 (frame 2, caption): then the website and the email stop
- Nominet, Registrar Resources (first URL above). Redemption Grace Period row, the second stage: "Does not resolve." A domain that does not resolve cannot serve a website or receive email.
- After 9 February 2027: Lifecycle Policy, section 9.2: "During the Redemption Grace Period, the registry disables DNS resolution and prohibits updates."

## Claim 3 (frame 2, caption): for a while you can still renew
- Nominet, Registrar Resources (first URL above). Redemption Grace Period row: available to renew. Pending Delete row: "Not available to renew."
- The UK Domain help page (second URL above), on the redemption period: "your domain name is expired but still exists, and you are still able to renew it with your registrar".

## Claim 4 (frame 3, caption): then it is deleted, and anyone can register it
- The UK Domain help page (second URL above): "Immediately after the drop time the domain name no longer exists and is available to anyone who wants to register it."

## Claim 5 (caption): Nominet runs .uk

  "We're the official registry for .UK". "we run .UK for the greater good".

## Claim 6 (caption): how long each stage lasts is down to Nominet and your registrar
- Nominet sets the periods: the Registrar Resources table above.
- The registrar: The UK Domain help page (second URL above) says a registrar's own terms "may result in your domain name and associated services being suspended before the times stated below".
- After the change: Nominet, Registrar Resources, "Legacy .UK to new .UK transition" (third source below): "Auto-renew domains will then act as per the registrars chosen system behaviour at the end of the auto-renew grace period."

## Claim 7 (caption): Nominet is moving .uk to a new system on 9 February 2027
- Nominet blog, "Date set for .UK Standardisation"  https://nominet.uk/blog/date-set-for-uk-standardisation/
  "We have now confirmed the biggest set of changes ... will take place on Tuesday, 9th February 2027."

  "The transition date has been confirmed as 9th February 2027".
- The UK Domain, "Changes to .UK domain processes"  https://theukdomain.uk/uk-standardisation/

## Claim 8 (caption): and the overall timeline gets shorter
- Nominet, Registrar Resources, "Legacy .UK to new .UK transition"  https://registrars.nominet.uk/registry/dot-uk/transition/
  Modified 21 July 2026. "This is a shortening of the lifecycle to match the new lifecycle for domains that have failed to be renewed." Names more than 75 days past expiry at the transition "will enter the RFC3915 pendingDelete period" and "cannot be restored"; names 30 to 75 days past expiry get a redemption period ending at "expiry plus 75 days". (Today a name can be renewed until 90 days past expiry.)

- Nominet, ".UK Registry-Registrar Lifecycle Policy", version 2026-03, "Policy effective from the 9th of February 2027"
  https://nominet.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/UK-Registry-Registrar-Lifecycle-Policy-2026-03.pdf
  "Auto-renew Grace Period: 45 days". "Redemption Grace Period: 30 days". "Pending Delete Grace Period: 5 days". That is 80 days from expiry to deletion at most, against 95 today. The same order of stages: still resolving (if the registrar leaves it on), then DNS disabled but restorable through the registrar (section 9.1), then pending delete, then "purge the domain from the registry" (section 5.4.3).

## Claim 9 (caption, implied): reminders are sent
- The UK Domain help page (second URL above): if the registrar does not renew before expiry, "we'll send an email to the contact email address we hold for the legal owner of the domain name".
- After 9 February 2027: Lifecycle Policy, section 6.1: registrars must notify registrants "Approximately one month prior to expiry", "Approximately one week prior to expiry", and at or within 5 days after expiry if not renewed.
