## 1. On a website a colour is often written as a hex code: a # and six characters
- Used on: scene 3 (the top leaf's band reads "#5B2A86"), caption ("On a website a colour is often written as a hex code, a # and six characters.").
- Source: MDN Web Docs (Mozilla), "<hex-color> CSS type". https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/hex-color (the page's own address is now https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/hex-color)
- As the source states it: the type is "a notation for describing the hexadecimal color syntax of an sRGB color using its primary color components (red, green, blue) written as hexadecimal numbers". Its syntax lists "#RGB", "#RGBA", "#RRGGBB" and "#RRGGBBAA". "The syntax is case-insensitive: #00ff00 is the same as #00FF00."
- Date: last modified 20 April 2026. Checked 14 Nov 2026.
- Scope: how colours are written in CSS, on any website, worldwide. The page does not say how common hex is, so the caption says "often", not "usually" or "always". The six-character form (#RRGGBB) is one of four the page lists. Source 2 also says: "A hexadecimal is made up of a hashtag and six character".

## 2. Printers mostly work in CMYK, or a Pantone number when the colour has to stay the same from job to job
- Used on: caption only ("Printers mostly work in CMYK, or a Pantone number when the colour has to stay the same from job to job.").
- Source: "CMYK RGB HEX PMS? Colour Spaces and Printmaking", on the LCC Printmaking, Book Arts and Letterpress blog (London College of Communication's printmaking workshops, hosted by University of the Arts London at myblog.arts.ac.uk). https://lccprintmaking.myblog.arts.ac.uk/2021/03/29/cmyk-rgb-hex-pms-colour-spaces-and-printmaking/
- As the source states it: "RGB is used for anything displayed on a screen". "Hexadecimals, which you may have come across in web design, are basically shorthand for RGB." "Digital printing and offset lithographic printing predominantly use CMYK for standard four-colour printing." The Pantone Matching System "is a numbering system for colours in graphic design", used "For more colour accuracy when printing, especially across different jobs, or when different printers being used" and "To define specific brand colours that can always be accurately reproduced through spot colour printing".
- Date: published 29 March 2021. Checked 14 Nov 2026. It is more than 24 months old; it is used for how print and screen colour work, which has not changed, and not for any number.
- Scope: general print practice, written for UK students. Not a statistic.

## 3. The NHS lists its blue as Pantone 300, hex #005EB8
- Used on: caption only ("The NHS lists its blue as Pantone 300, hex #005EB8.").
- Source: NHS England, NHS identity guidelines, "Colours". https://england.nhs.uk/nhsidentity/identity-guidelines/colours
- As the source states it: NHS Blue is given as "Pantone: 300", "CMYK: 99/50/0/0", "RGB: 0/94/184", "#005EB8" and "RAL: 5017". The page says "Colour is a key element of any organisation's identity."
- Date: no date is shown on the page. Checked 14 Nov 2026 (live).
- Scope: the NHS in England's own identity guidelines. Used as an example of a brand colour written down as codes. No NHS logo or NHS colour appears in the Reel.

## 4. The fan deck
- The colour fan deck is a 3D render made for this post (art/build.py, art/cards.py): five leaves on a rivet, one made-up logo colour and four near misses. Every leaf is printed "Purple." with where that purple ended up (LOGO, VAN, SIGN, WEBSITE, UNIFORM). In scene 3 the top leaf reads "#5B2A86" with "This purple." printed across its colour. It is not a real product or a real paint or ink range, and no business is named. The caption says it is made up.
- The pictures are graded so that the top leaf's colour measures its code in the picture file (the render script reads #5A2B84 at its probe, against #5B2A86). A phone screen will still show it its own way, and nothing in the post says otherwise.
