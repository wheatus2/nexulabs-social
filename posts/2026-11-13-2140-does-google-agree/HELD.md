**The idea.** Opening hours on Google. A photograph of a shop door whose own sign says OPEN; under it, as the sign's next two lines in the sign's own cream and cut to its width: "DOES GOOGLE AGREE?" Then "Search your business name. Check the hours."

**The three reviews: 6, 5.5, 6** (pass mark 7). All three rated the idea highly ("an 8", "lands in one read") and marked the execution down.
1. Question set as window lettering in place of the shop's own "SHOP" (pale serif, on the window's own rule): 6. "Basic: photo, one line, logo." The lettering followed the window's line, not the sign, and read as a small crooked caption.
2. Question hung under the sign at the sign's tilt, in tall condensed capitals: 5.5. The retouched top of the photo was the fault (a pale disc painted out, cords left cut off in mid-air, a ghost where a lamp was toned down).
3. Photo levelled on the sign, top cropped instead of retouched, two-line headline cut to the sign's width: 6. "The idea is an 8; the bottom half is a template: photo, dark scrim, condensed caps, small logo."

**What they all said.** A library photo at this size is soft and a little noisy next to crisp type, and photo plus scrim plus headline plus logo is a formula however well it is fitted. See DESIGN.md.

**If it is reworked.** Keep the idea. It needs a sharper photograph of a sign, or the sign drawn or built for the post, and type that is part of the picture, not laid over a darkened half.
