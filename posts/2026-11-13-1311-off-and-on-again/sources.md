# Sources

Post: 2026-11-13-1311-off-and-on-again (carousel, 3 frames). Lee asked for this post in the chat on 13 Nov 2026: the Microsoft outage, with some humour ("have they tried to turn it off then on again"). Every page below was opened on 13 Nov 2026 in this run, each after a web search returned its URL.

**Not opened first-hand:** Microsoft's own status page (status.cloud.microsoft) loads only a JavaScript shell, and the Microsoft 365 Status posts on X would not open. So Microsoft's words below are quoted as reported by the outlets named, which is why the caption says "its status updates said".

## 1. Microsoft 365 went down on Thursday 12 Nov 2026, with Outlook, Teams and more affected
- Used on: frame 1 ("Microsoft 365 went down on Thursday." "Outlook, Teams and more."), caption ("Microsoft 365 went down on Thursday and took Outlook and Teams with it.").
- Source: SecurityWeek, "Microsoft 365 Outage Disrupts Outlook, Exchange Online and Teams", 12 Nov 2026. https://www.securityweek.com/microsoft-365-outage-disrupts-outlook-exchange-online-and-teams/ : Microsoft 365 "suffered a widespread outage Thursday"; users "unable to connect to Exchange Online mailboxes"; "potential disruption to Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Defender XDR".
- Second source: WLKY (Hearst Television), "Microsoft says some Outlook servers have service restored after outage", 12 Nov 2026, updated 6:19pm EST. https://www.wlky.com/article/microsoft-outlook-not-working-outage/74414280 : "As of 1:15 p.m., downdetector.com showed nearly 130,000 people had reported problems."
- Third source: Evening Standard live blog (as republished by AOL UK), updated Friday 13 Nov 2026. https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/microsoft-outage-live-outlook-hotmail-063847000.html : UK users affected from about 6.15pm on Thursday; "Downdetector recording disruption in more than 70 countries."
- No number is used in the post.

## 2. Microsoft said it was restarting the affected servers
- Used on: frame 2 ("Microsoft did.", "From Microsoft’s status updates, 12 Nov. Yes, really." and the quote “We’re actively restarting and validating the affected servers to return them to service.”), caption ("restarting things was on the list: its status updates said it was "actively restarting and validating the affected servers"").
- Source: WLKY, the same article, its 4:40pm EST update on 12 Nov 2026, quoting Microsoft: "We're actively restarting and validating the affected servers to return them to service."
- Second source: Cyber Security News, "Microsoft 365 Massive Outage - Fixes Fail as Restored Servers Keep Going Unresponsive", 13 Nov 2026 06:20 UTC. https://cybersecuritynews.com/microsoft-365-massive-outage-fixes-fail-as-restored-servers-keep-going-unresponsive/ , quoting the Microsoft 365 Status account: "positive results with our efforts to restart affected infrastructure and recover server availability."
- The joke on frame 1 ("Have you tried turning it off and on again?") is a joke, not a claim. Frame 2 is the true part: restarting servers was one of the things Microsoft said it was doing. It was not the only thing (it also reverted a recent network configuration change and applied a patch), and the post does not say it was.

## 3. Some restarted servers went down again, and the outage was still being fixed on Friday
- Used on: frame 3 (headline "Some then went down again."), caption ("Some then fell over again, and it was still being put right on Friday.").
- Source: Cyber Security News, the same article, quoting Microsoft: restored servers "can become unresponsive again once they receive traffic." The article: "Microsoft has yet to find a fix that holds for the global Microsoft 365 outage."
- Second source: Evening Standard live blog (AOL UK), Friday 13 Nov 2026: Microsoft got servers "operating multiple times overnight UK time, those servers had crashed"; at 12:27 on Friday Microsoft said services were "being gradually restored."
- Checked again at about 13:15 UK time on Friday 13 Nov: search results from ABC News, CNN and Windows Central still described the outage as continuing, with "progress toward recovery". The post says "still being put right on Friday" and does not say it is fixed.

## 4. Advice: have a second way for customers to reach you
- Used on: frame 3 ("Got a plan B for when your email is down?" "A phone number on your website is a start."), caption (last paragraph).
- Our own advice. No outside fact is claimed.
- Care taken after the first fact-check: a contact form that only emails the enquiry to the mailbox that is down does not help (the Evening Standard blog reports online forms were affected at some organisations in this outage). So the frame talks about the phone, and the caption says "a contact form that saves each enquiry as well as emailing it".
