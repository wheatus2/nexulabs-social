# Sources

Story: a business card for a made-up joiner whose email address is a Gmail one, then the same card with the email on the firm's own web address. Quick tip, Domain names. Checked 13 Nov 2026.

## Words on the frames

1. "Nice card. Shame about the last line."
2. "The card says Coble & Keel. The email says Gmail."
3. "Same name, all the way down. Put your email on your web address. Ask whoever set up your website."

## Claims

There is no statistic, quote, client or result on any frame. Frames 1 and 2 only describe the made-up card in the picture.

## Advice on frame 3

"Put your email on your web address" is advice, not a claim. It matches UK trade press guidance:

- Startups.co.uk, "How to create a business email address: step-by-step guide", by Emily Clark. Published 2 Jan 2019, updated 9 Jun 2026. URL: https://startups.co.uk/websites/how-to-set-up-a-business-email/ (found by web search and opened in this run, 13 Nov 2026).
  - As the page states it, under "Use a professional domain": "avoid free providers like Gmail, Yahoo or Hotmail for business emails". Step 1 says the domain is "the part that comes after the '@' in your email address".
  - The same page says "Most web hosting providers offer custom email services with domain registration", which is why frame 3 says to ask whoever set up the website. The frame does not promise that it is included or free.
