# Sources

Story: 99% uptime against 99.9% uptime, drawn as a year-long ring with a gap. Checked 12 Nov 2026.

## Claims on the frames

1. Claim (frames 1 and 2): 99% uptime leaves 1% of the year offline, which is 3.65 days ("3.65 days", "1% of the year. Your site, offline.").
   - Arithmetic: 1% of 365 days = 3.65 days (87.6 hours).
   - Source: "High availability", Wikipedia. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_availability (found by web search and opened in this run, 12 Nov 2026; the page shows no publish date).
   - As the page states it: its "Percentage calculation" table gives, for 99% availability, downtime per year of "3.7 days" (rounded). The page says "Availability is usually expressed as a percentage of uptime in a given year" and works from "8760 hours per non-leap year".

2. Claim (frame 3): 99.9% uptime is "Under nine hours a year" offline.
   - Arithmetic: 0.1% of 8,760 hours = 8.76 hours (8 hours 46 minutes).
   - Source: the same Wikipedia table gives, for 99.9%, downtime per year of "9 hours" (rounded), and the text gives 99.9% as "8751 hours of available time out of 8760 hours per non-leap year", which is 9 hours down at most. The frame says "under nine hours", which matches the exact figure of 8.76.

3. Background, UK source (not quoted on the frames): Krystal, a UK web host, "Understanding bandwidth, storage and other tech specs without being an expert", by Darren H, 9 Jul 2025. URL: https://krystal.io/blog/post/understanding-bandwidth-storage-and-other-tech-specs-without-being-an-expert (found by web search and opened in this run).
   - As the page states it: "Uptime measures what proportion of each hour/day/month your website is accessible." and "Even 99.9% uptime can equal up to 43 minutes of downtime per month." 43 minutes a month is about 8.7 hours a year, which agrees with claim 2.

## Advice on the frames

"Ask your host which is yours." is advice, not a claim. Hosts state uptime as a percentage (see the Krystal page above), so it is a number a business can ask for.

## The drawing

The ring on frame 1 is drawn to scale: 99% of the circle is lit and the gap is 1% (3.6 degrees), shown lying flat in perspective. The cut in the arc on frame 3 is 0.1% of the circle (about 0.4 degrees). Frame 2 is the 1% gap enlarged, at the same proportion of gap to ring width as frame 1.
