## Advice on the frames

1. Claim: hyphens and numbers make a web address harder to say and pass on ("No hyphens. No numbers.").
   - Source: "How to choose a domain name", theukdomain.uk (author shown as Zoe Brown; the page shows no publish date and does not name its publisher).
   - URL: https://theukdomain.uk/get-online/how-to-choose-a-domain-name/ (found by web search and opened in this run, 12 Nov 2026).
   - As the page states it: its list of things to avoid includes "Hyphens" and "Numbers – these cause confusion because, when spoken, it’s not clear whether a number should be spelled out". It also says a name "needs to be easy to remember, easy to say and easy to type" and asks "Easy to say? – does it trip off the tongue?"

2. Claim: "Four, for or 4? Out loud, they sound the same."
   - Plain English: the words are said the same way. It matches the source's point above about numbers when spoken.

Self-check: nothing on the frames goes beyond the source. No statistics, clients, quotes or results are used.
